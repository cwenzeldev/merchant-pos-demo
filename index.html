Lead with the merchant.

A three-minute sales demonstration with technical depth available when asked.

1 / Discover the friction

“During your busiest shift, where does service slow down most: taking orders, handling guest requests, or closing checks?”

2 / Show a relevant workflow

Add a burger and a drink, capture a guest request, and split the check. Explain how fewer handoffs could help the team serve guests more efficiently. Ask: “How does your team handle this today?”

3 / Demonstrate recovery and visibility

Simulate a decline, show that the order stays intact, then complete a sale. Open Merchant insights to show sales, tax, and tips separately.

4 / Earn the next step

“If we could simplify this workflow for your staff, would it make sense to review your current setup and identify what an implementation would require?”

Technical talking points

Order → payment → reporting. An order captures items and guest requests. Only a successful simulated payment creates a completed transaction.

Reliable amounts. Prices and calculations use whole cents. Split checks allocate remaining cents so guest shares add up exactly.

Payment integration. A real solution would use a supported terminal and processor integration, verify payment results on a server, and prevent duplicate charges. This prototype does not connect to a processor.

Authorization and settlement. Authorization approves a payment request; settlement moves funds later. The demo models a completed sale without implementing that lifecycle.

Deployment scope. This is a browser application with local storage. Shared locations, staff permissions, live kitchen routing, and offline payment processing would require additional infrastructure.

Independent portfolio prototype. No Shift4 affiliation or integration is implied. No card data is collected.
